# Public-set results, all experiments to 2026-11-06 PLCC/SRCC

One table with every model evaluated on the public sets so far, compared with MVQA (the target), and one section per row
that explains how that row's model was trained and evaluated. Earlier pages: [`PUBLIC_2026-11-06.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-06.md),
[`PUBLIC_2026-11-04.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-04.md).

## How to read the table

- Every cell is **PLCC/SRCC**; PLCC is computed after a 4-parameter logistic fit. MVQA numbers are copied from Table 1 of
  arXiv 2609.16946 (MVQA reports no YouTube-UGC cross-dataset number and no parameter or FLOP count we could verify: n/r).
- Colour key: $\color{green}{\mathbf{green}}$ = best in the column, $\color{blue}{\mathbf{blue}}$ = second best; PLCC
  and SRCC are ranked separately.
- **x** = cross-dataset: the regressor is fit on LSVQ train only and applied unchanged. **FT** = a regressor fit on each
  dataset's own training part, median of 10 random 70/10/20 splits (MVQA fully fine-tunes per dataset, so FT is not exactly
  like-for-like).
- **Params** = parameters needed at inference. **TFLOPs/clip** = inference compute for an average LSVQ clip (218 frames,
  about 7 s), measured with PyTorch's FLOP counter on the real modules and the real input shapes (FLOPs = 2 x multiply-adds);
  the SVR is negligible. Rows evaluated at native and half scale count both passes.
- Row numbers are stable identifiers; rows 21 and above were added after the first version of the table.

## Each experiment

### Row 1: DINOv2-S, raw public weights

**Model.** DINOv2-S (facebook/dinov2-small, ViT-S/14, public weights) applied to 256x256 crops of the native-resolution frame (up to 32 fixed-seed grid cells, one tile frame every 15 native frames). Nothing is trained by us.

**Evaluation.** Each tile gives one DINOv2-S embedding (384 values); per clip we keep their mean and standard deviation over all tiles (768 values) at native resolution. An RBF-kernel SVR maps the features to MOS. Its C and gamma are chosen by grid search on a fixed 8,000-clip subset of LSVQ train and the SVR is then refit on all 28,056 LSVQ-train clips. That SVR scores LSVQ test (7,186 clips) and LSVQ-1080p (3,573 clips), and is applied unchanged to KoNViD-1k (1,200), LIVE-VQC (585) and YouTube-UGC (977) for the cross-dataset (x) columns. For the FT columns a separate SVR is fit on each dataset's own training part, over 10 random 70/10/20 splits, and the median test score is reported.

### Row 2: VideoMAE-v2-B, raw public weights

**Model.** VideoMAE-v2-B (ViT-B/16, Kinetics-710 fine-tuned, distilled from ViT-g; public weights). Nothing is trained by us.

**Evaluation.** V2-B is run on every displayed frame area-downscaled to fit 384x216 (never upscaled), padded to 224 rows; every 64-frame span of the clip gives 16 frames (every 4th frame), and per span we keep [final-layer token mean, token std] and [block-6 token mean, token std]; the spans are averaged ("last+mid" features, 3,072 values). An RBF-kernel SVR maps the features to MOS. Its C and gamma are chosen by grid search on a fixed 8,000-clip subset of LSVQ train and the SVR is then refit on all 28,056 LSVQ-train clips. That SVR scores LSVQ test (7,186 clips) and LSVQ-1080p (3,573 clips), and is applied unchanged to KoNViD-1k (1,200), LIVE-VQC (585) and YouTube-UGC (977) for the cross-dataset (x) columns. For the FT columns a separate SVR is fit on each dataset's own training part, over 10 random 70/10/20 splits, and the median test score is reported.

### Row 3: Raw DINOv2-S + raw VideoMAE-v2-B features

**Model.** Two public encoders with no training by us: DINOv2-S (facebook/dinov2-small, ViT-S/14, public weights) applied to 256x256 crops of the native-resolution frame (up to 32 fixed-seed grid cells, one tile frame every 15 native frames), and VideoMAE-v2-B (ViT-B/16, Kinetics-710 fine-tuned, distilled from ViT-g; public weights).

**Evaluation.** The DINOv2-S tile embeddings give [mean, std] over all tiles (768 values); V2-B is run on every displayed frame area-downscaled to fit 384x216 (never upscaled), padded to 224 rows; every 64-frame span of the clip gives 16 frames (every 4th frame), and per span we keep [final-layer token mean, token std] and [block-6 token mean, token std]; the spans are averaged ("last+mid" features, 3,072 values). The two vectors are concatenated. An RBF-kernel SVR maps the features to MOS. Its C and gamma are chosen by grid search on a fixed 8,000-clip subset of LSVQ train and the SVR is then refit on all 28,056 LSVQ-train clips. That SVR scores LSVQ test (7,186 clips) and LSVQ-1080p (3,573 clips), and is applied unchanged to KoNViD-1k (1,200), LIVE-VQC (585) and YouTube-UGC (977) for the cross-dataset (x) columns. For the FT columns a separate SVR is fit on each dataset's own training part, over 10 random 70/10/20 splits, and the median test score is reported.

### Row 4: Stage A, frozen encoders + LSVQ-tuned fusion

**Model.** S1 = our Tile-MAE (ViT-S) trained with a masked-autoencoder objective on 256x256 native-resolution tiles of our synthetic training data: 7,068 4K 60 fps SDR source clips (about 5 s each). For every source we make a 5-rung compression (rate) ladder, rendered capture-distortion versions at 5 visually verified severity levels, and on-the-fly frame-drop versions in four verified families (A1 single freezes of 10-60 frames, A2 1-8 freezes of 15 frames, B frame rate reduced to 30/15/7.5 or 20/10 fps by sample-and-hold, B2 random holds at about 36-6 fps) (clean and rendered versions; no frame drops). S2 = our Stage A motion encoder S2: VideoMAE-S initialised from public SSv2 weights, then trained 100k steps with masked-video reconstruction on 384x216 32-frame windows of our clean and rendered synthetic versions (no frame drops). Fusion = a 4-layer transformer fusion head (width 256, 4 heads) that takes S1 tile tokens (with tile-grid position) and S2 motion tokens (with time and region position) in 4-second windows, pools them with attention queries and outputs a quality score per window, which are combined into one clip score. **Training.** The fusion head is first trained on our synthetic compression and rendering-severity pairs (the worse version must score lower), then fine-tuned on LSVQ-train MOS with 1 - PLCC + 0.3 x rank hinge. Both encoders stay frozen; no MOS ever reaches the encoders.

**Evaluation.** The trained model is frozen and run twice per clip: on the native-resolution video and on the video downscaled by 2 inside the decoder. From each run we keep the fusion head's pooled clip embedding and the mean and standard deviation of the S1 and S2 tokens; the native and half-scale vectors are concatenated ("fusion+S1S2, multi-scale").  An RBF-kernel SVR maps the features to MOS. Its C and gamma are chosen by grid search on a fixed 8,000-clip subset of LSVQ train and the SVR is then refit on all 28,056 LSVQ-train clips. That SVR scores LSVQ test (7,186 clips) and LSVQ-1080p (3,573 clips), and is applied unchanged to KoNViD-1k (1,200), LIVE-VQC (585) and YouTube-UGC (977) for the cross-dataset (x) columns. For the FT columns a separate SVR is fit on each dataset's own training part, over 10 random 70/10/20 splits, and the median test score is reported.

### Row 5: Stage A, end-to-end on LSVQ (6k steps)

**Model.** S1 = our Tile-MAE (ViT-S, masked-autoencoder trained on 256x256 tiles of our clean and rendered synthetic 4K60 versions), S2 = our Stage A motion encoder S2: VideoMAE-S initialised from public SSv2 weights, then trained 100k steps with masked-video reconstruction on 384x216 32-frame windows of our clean and rendered synthetic versions (no frame drops), fusion = a 4-layer transformer fusion head (width 256, 4 heads) that takes S1 tile tokens (with tile-grid position) and S2 motion tokens (with time and region position) in 4-second windows, pools them with attention queries and outputs a quality score per window, which are combined into one clip score, initialised from the synthetic-pairs + LSVQ fusion. **Training.** All three parts are trained end to end on LSVQ train MOS: every step takes 8 random 128-frame windows of LSVQ-train videos, the loss is 1 - PLCC + 0.3 x pairwise rank hinge on the predicted scores, AdamW with cosine schedule and 5% warm-up, encoder learning rate 2e-5 for the top block with layer-wise decay 0.75 per block below it, fusion learning rate 3e-5, for 6,000 steps, without downscale augmentation.
