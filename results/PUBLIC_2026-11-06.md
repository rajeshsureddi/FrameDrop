# Public-set results, 2026-11-06: where we stand PLCC/SRCC

**Purpose of this page:** one table with every model we have evaluated on the public sets so far, from untouched public
encoders up to our current best models, so the effect of each step is visible in one place. The external reference is
MVQA, the model we are trying to beat. Earlier pages: [`PUBLIC_2026-11-04.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-04.md),
[`PUBLIC_2026-11-03.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-03.md), [`PUBLIC_2026-11-02.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-02.md).

## How to read the table

- Every cell is **PLCC/SRCC**. PLCC is computed after a 4-parameter logistic fit.
- Colour key: $\color{green}{\mathbf{green}}$ = best in the column, $\color{blue}{\mathbf{blue}}$ = second best. PLCC and
  SRCC are ranked separately; ties share a colour.
- **Same protocol for every row:** the model is frozen and used as a feature extractor; an SVR is fit on top of the
  features. Where a model has a trained fusion head we use the fusion output plus the pooled encoder features (S1, S2), at
  native and half resolution combined (multi-scale).
- **LSVQ test / LSVQ 1080p:** SVR fit on LSVQ train, tested on the two official LSVQ test sets.
- **x (cross-dataset):** the same LSVQ-fit SVR applied unchanged to KoNViD-1k, LIVE-VQC and YouTube-UGC.
- **FT:** SVR fit per dataset on the frozen features, median over 10 random 70/10/20 splits. MVQA fully fine-tunes per
  dataset, so FT columns are not exactly like-for-like. MVQA does not report YouTube-UGC cross-dataset.
- MVQA numbers are copied from Table 1 of arXiv 2609.16946.

## What each group of rows is

**A. Raw public encoders (rows 1–3).** The public checkpoints exactly as released, nothing trained by us:
DINOv2-S (an image model; per-frame spatial features) and VideoMAE-v2-B (V2-B, a video model; motion features). This is
the floor every later step must beat.

**B. Stage A (rows 4–6).** Our first design: our own spatial encoder (Tile-MAE, S1) and motion encoder (V-MAE, S2),
pre-trained only on our synthetic distortion data (no human labels), then a fusion head trained on LSVQ MOS.
Row 4 keeps the encoders frozen; row 5 then trains everything end to end on LSVQ; row 6 trains end to end longer with
downscale augmentation (random 1.25–2x downscaling of training clips).

**C. DINOv2-S as S1 (rows 7–9).** Same as Stage A, but the spatial encoder S1 is replaced by raw DINOv2-S; the motion
encoder is still our Stage A S2. Row 7 (`dino_raw`): fusion trained on LSVQ, then 6k steps end to end. Row 8: the same
without the motion branch. Row 9 (`dla`, dino_long_aug): 12k steps end to end with downscale augmentation; our best
trained model until 11-05.

**D. V2-B trained by us (rows 10–13).** The public V2-B, further trained by us, then frozen and probed:
- *synthetic pairs only* (row 10): trained only to rank our synthetic distortion pairs (frame rate and severity levels),
  no MOS; layer-wise lr decay 0.75 so early layers move little.
- *LSVQ MOS only* (row 11): trained only on LSVQ MOS (ReLIQS ranking loss, all layers, lr 2e-5, 6k steps).
- rows 12–13: the same two, combined with raw DINOv2-S features.

**E. dla + V2-B (rows 14–17).** Features of our best model `dla` concatenated with V2-B features, one SVR on top. This
tells us how much a V2-B motion branch could add to `dla`. *jointP4* = V2-B trained on LSVQ MOS with our synthetic pairs
mixed in on every 4th step.

**F. Full model (rows 18–20).** A single trained model: raw DINOv2-S (spatial) + synthetic-trained V2-B (motion) + a
fusion head trained on LSVQ MOS.
- *Frozen* (rows 18–19): encoders frozen, only the fusion head trained. Model A uses the row-10 V2-B (lr decay 0.75);
  Model B uses a V2-B trained on synthetic pairs with all layers at lr 2e-5.
- *Model A e2e* (row 20): Model A after training all parts together on LSVQ MOS for 12k steps with downscale
  augmentation (the `dla` recipe).

## What the table says

1. **Raw encoders are a strong floor.** Raw DINOv2-S + V2-B (row 3) already reaches 0.889/0.888 on LSVQ test.
2. **Swapping our spatial encoder for DINOv2-S was the largest single gain:** Stage A (rows 4–6) stays near .85 on LSVQ
   test; with DINOv2-S as S1 (rows 7–9) it rises to about .895.
3. **Synthetic-only V2-B training gives a small gain:** +.001 to +.013 over raw V2-B alone (row 10 vs row 2; largest on the FT columns), and about
   zero once added to `dla` (row 15 vs row 14).
4. **V2-B trained on LSVQ MOS is the strongest motion branch:** alone it beats raw V2-B on every column (row 11 vs row 2).
   Added to `dla` (row 16), it beats MVQA on 4 of 7 columns: LSVQ test, LSVQ 1080p, KoNViD x and LIVE-VQC FT. Mixing in
   synthetic pairs (row 17) changes results only within noise.
5. **Still behind MVQA** on LIVE-VQC x (best −.012 PLCC / −.020 SRCC, row 13), KoNViD FT (−.010 / −.013) and YouTube-UGC FT
   (−.006 / −.007).
6. **End-to-end training of the full model helps a lot:** Model A goes from row 18 to row 20 (+.026 / +.027 LSVQ test),
   reaching the level of the `dla` + V2-B probes on LSVQ and 1080p, but not on KoNViD.
