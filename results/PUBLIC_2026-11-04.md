# Public-set results, 2026-11-04 PLCC/SRCC

**Question of this page:** does training on our synthetic data (no human labels) make a video model better at quality? We
test this on public VideoMAE-v2 backbones and compare everything with the HFVQA Table 1 baselines. Earlier pages:
[`PUBLIC_2026-11-03.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-03.md), [`PUBLIC_2026-11-02.md`](PUBLIC_2026-11-02.md).

All cells are **PLCC/SRCC**, the order of HFVQA Table 1. Our PLCC is computed after a 4-parameter logistic fit. Baseline rows
are copied from Table 1 of arXiv 2609.16946.

Colour key: $\color{green}{\mathbf{green}}$ = best, $\color{blue}{\mathbf{blue}}$ = second best. Ranking is per column, with
PLCC and SRCC ranked separately; ties share a colour. In table 1, $\color{orange}{orange}$ = our other numbers.

Columns:
- **x** = cross-dataset: the regressor is fit on LSVQ train only and applied unchanged.
- **FT** = SVR/Ridge fit per dataset on frozen features, median over 10 random 70/10/20 splits. MVQA fully fine-tunes per
  dataset, so FT columns are not exactly like-for-like.
- **dla** = our best LSVQ-trained model (`dino_long_aug`), used frozen as a feature extractor at native and half scale.

## Key findings

1. **Our synthetic data teaches a public video model quality.** Trained only on our synthetic pairs, with no human labels,
   VideoMAE-v2-S improves on every column: +.03 to +.08 SRCC with SVR, +.04 to +.07 with Ridge. The largest gains are on the
   per-dataset columns and LIVE-VQC, which have real capture distortions.
2. **The gain adds to DINOv2** (+.01 to +.04 SRCC), so it is information DINOv2 does not already have.
3. **DINOv2 must be left out of the synthetic stage.** With DINOv2 in the head the gain is only about +.01, and the model
   learns the synthetic task worse (rate .66, severity .66 vs 1.00 / .94), because the pairs can be solved through DINOv2.
4. **Middle layers matter.** Public V2-S goes from .803 to .870 LSVQ SRCC when block-6 features are added. Table 2 uses only
   the final layer; a final + middle version for the synthetic-trained models is running.
5. **On top of the LSVQ-trained dla, frozen add-ons give only +.002 to +.009.** Whether the gain survives inside a full model
   is tested by Stage 3 (running): both encoders frozen, fusion trained on LSVQ.
6. **V2-B beats V2-S** by about .01 standalone and learns the synthetic task faster; its synthetic training is running.

## How everything was trained

### Data

- **Synthetic training data:**
  - **Sources:** 4K60 SDR source spans (~7.4k sources, including nature-1k).
  - **Clean ladder:** 5 clean encodes per source at 5 rate rungs (compression only).
  - **Capture distortions:** a GPU-rendered camera/capture distortion plan at severities 0.5 and 1.0, each encoded at 2 rate
    rungs. All distortion levels were visually checked before use.
  - **Frame drops:** applied on the fly at training time as frame-index remaps. Families: burst, Bernoulli,
    Gilbert-Elliott, pacing and mixtures.
  - **GameVerse:** 746 4K60 gaming clips, drops only, mixed into the pair stream with probability .33. Every franchise in our
    user study is excluded.
- **Synthetic pair labels** (no human scores):
  - **Rate:** same source, the lower rate rung is better.
  - **Severity:** same capture plan and rate rung, the lower severity is better.
  - **Drops:** a drop pattern P vs a superset of P, same source; more drops is worse.
- **Public MOS sets:** used as is, at native resolution and frame rate, never upscaled.
  - **Training:** LSVQ train (28,056 clips) is the only MOS data used for training.
  - **Testing only:** LSVQ test / 1080p, KoNViD-1k, LIVE-VQC and YouTube-UGC. 41,577 clips in total.

### Encoders

- **DINOv2-S:** public facebook/dinov2-small, applied to 256×256 tiles cut from native-resolution frames (252 crop, patch
  14), one tile set every 15th frame, up to 32 tiles.
- **Our motion encoder (Stage A S2):** VideoMAE-S (SSv2 init), trained with MAE reconstruction only on 384×216 low-res
  windows of our synthetic data (no drops, no MOS).
- **Tile-MAE:** our Stage A spatial encoder, an MAE trained on 256×256 tiles of the synthetic data.
- **VideoMAE-v2-S / -B (public):** OpenGVLab ViT-S/16 and ViT-B/16, Kinetics-710, distilled from ViT-g; no quality labels.
  Input: our 384×216 view padded to 224×384 (pretraining used 224×224), position table resized.

### Fusion model and our trained models

- **Fusion:** a transformer over the spatial tile tokens and the motion tokens (16 time steps × 3×4 regions), with a quality
  head Q and a drop head V.
- **fusion_a:** fusion trained on frozen Stage A encoders with synthetic rate + severity pairs only (30k steps, no MOS).
- **33c:** fusion_a fine-tuned on LSVQ train MOS: (1 − PLCC) + 0.3 rank hinge, plus pairs, 20k steps.
- **dino_raw:** raw DINOv2-S + our motion encoder.
  1. Frozen tokens, then a fusion fine-tune from 33c for 20k steps (MOS only, (1 − PLCC) + rank).
  2. End-to-end LSVQ fine-tune for 6k steps (MOS only).
- **dla (dino_long_aug):** the same as dino_raw, but the end-to-end stage runs for 12k steps with downscale augmentation (p
  .3, ×1.25–2).
  - 8 LSVQ windows per step.
  - Learning rates: encoders 2e-5 (layer-wise decay .75), fusion 3e-5.
- **stageB_alt_gv:** the Tile-MAE + our motion encoder model trained end to end on LSVQ MOS, alternating with Stage B
  synthetic pairs (drops + rate + severity, GameVerse mixed in) every 4th step.

### Synthetic training of VideoMAE-v2 (Step 42)

- **"Alone" runs (V2-S `v2o_syn`, V2-B `v2ob_syn`):**
  - **Start:** public VideoMAE-v2 weights.
  - **Head:** small. Q = MLP on the [mean, std] of all motion tokens; V = a per-time-step score pooled with logsumexp.
  - **Pairs:** synthetic only (drops + rate + severity, GameVerse p .33); 6,000 steps × 4 pairs.
  - **Loss:** rank loss on Q, drop loss on V, and 0.1 × invariance.
  - **Learning rates:** encoder 2e-5 (layer-wise decay .75), head 3e-5.
  - **Input:** every 2nd frame of each 32-frame window, 224×384.
  - **Cost:** V2-S took 2.8 h on one A100.
- **"DINOv2 in head" run (`v2f_syn`):** the same pairs and schedule, but frozen raw DINOv2-S tile tokens and V2-S both feed our
  fusion model (initialised from fusion_a).
- **No MOS labels** are used in any of these runs.

### Features and regressors

- **Table 2:** [mean, std] of the final-layer (fc_norm) motion tokens over the 3×4 regions, pooled over the clip.
  DINOv2-S = [mean, std] of its tile tokens.
- **Table 3:** the features described in section 3.
- **dla features:** the pooled fusion feature plus the encoders' [mean, std] tokens, at native and half scale.
- **Regressors:** SVR and Ridge with the same protocol code as all earlier pages (step30_fit).
